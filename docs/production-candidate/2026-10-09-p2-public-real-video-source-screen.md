## Result

The source review found genre-diverse public examples with file-level license labels, but no currently admitted P2 dataset or holdout. Most examples are single edited clips; their licenses do not establish permission for this product's local or cloud AI processing, retention, derived-feature storage, or publication. No unrelated clips are being combined and treated as one event.

The strongest lead for a bounded real-event, multi-file diagnostic is NASA SVS 14948, a Roman Space Telescope integration event with multiple views, including two no-audio files. NASA's own guidance distinguishes general copyright status from commercial use, identifiable-person rights, third-party content, endorsement, and AI attribution. The relevant NASA files therefore remain conditional and not admitted.

## Rights and evidence rules

For each exact file, keep these claims separate:

1. The item page's stated source, authorship, license, credits, file version, and download details.
2. Other rights in recognizable people, music/audio, marks, locations, and incorporated third-party material.
3. Director Brain's permission for the exact local or third-party route, including copying, decoding, caching, frame/feature derivation, model processing, retention/deletion, and any later publication or commercial use.

Wikimedia Commons says the applicable license is on each file's description page. It summarizes CC BY as allowing commercial reuse/adaptation with attribution and CC BY-SA as adding a share-alike condition. Commons also says non-copyright restrictions such as personality, trademark, and moral rights remain the reuser's responsibility. A Commons file page is therefore not project-owner permission or an all-rights clearance. See [Commons reuse guidance](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Reusing_content_outside_Wikimedia/licenses/en) and [non-copyright restrictions](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Non-copyright_restrictions).

NASA's current [Images and Media Usage Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/) state that NASA media is generally not subject to U.S. copyright, but NASA may host third-party material whose rights do not transfer. Its commercial-use guidance addresses endorsement and identifiable people; its AI section says NASA attribution cannot be presented as validation of model outputs and that disclosing NASA source material does not imply NASA reviewed or permitted a specific use. These rules are not a blanket grant for a commercial AI product's data route.
